    Join the Lab

    Thank you for your interest in joining the ADCL! We receive many inquiries about graduate student positions. Prof. Sunberg reviews all applications that mention him and typically conducts a few interviews in January or February, so you do not need to email him.

    Prospective PhD Students

    Prof. Sunberg will be on sabbatical in academic year 2027-2028, so he is unlikely to accept new PhD students for Fall 2027.

    If you would like to be considered for any positions that do open up with Prof. Sunberg or his colleagues, please apply to the Aerospace Engineering Sciences or Robotics PhD program and mention him as a preferred advisor:

    • How to apply: Aerospace Engineering Sciences
    • How to apply: Robotics

    You may email Professor Sunberg, but due to the large number of inquiries, he may not be able to respond.

    Prospective MS Students

    Prof. Sunberg generally has limited capacity to fund or serve as primary thesis advisor for MS students. In most cases, the MS students he has advised have taken and done well in his class, ASEN 5264: Decision Making under Uncertainty.

    Prospective Undergraduate Researchers

    We have limited ability to support undergraduate researchers. However, part of our mission is to help undergraduates find their place in the engineering ecosystem, so we encourage undergraduates to ask their professors about research. The best way to get started with undergraduate research is through the Summer Program for Undergraduate Research (CU SPUR) or the academic-year Discovery Learning Apprenticeship (DLA) program. Please reach out to Professor Sunberg via email or come to office hours if you are an undergraduate at CU interested in doing research with the ADCL. Include a resume and transcript if you send an email.

    Prof. Sunberg’s three pieces of advice for prospective undergraduate researchers:

    1. Work hard in your courses. Grades are not a perfect way to assess skills, but they are one of the only indicators we have for deciding who to work with in research. Going deeper with course projects is a particularly good way to show your interest and skills.
    2. Go to office hours and get to know the professors you want to work with.
    3. Get involved in engineering clubs, like the sounding rocket club or whatever you are most interested in. This can help you demonstrate and hone your skills.
